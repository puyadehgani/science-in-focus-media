Can a tumour be removed endoscopically, through the nose? 🔬 Part 4/5 · History of FESS

Before endoscopy: tumours were reached through the face (lateral rhinotomy, described by Moure in 1902, later modified; then the midfacial degloving) and, for skull base cancer, through the skull as well (craniofacial resection, 1963).

1️⃣ Benign tumours: from 1992, inverted papillomas were removed endoscopically, with recurrence similar to open surgery; the endoscopic medial maxillectomy (1995) removed the inner wall of the maxillary sinus. Juvenile angiofibromas followed, reached through the opened maxillary sinus up to the pterygopalatine fossa.
2️⃣ Skull base cancer: first the cranio-endoscopic approach (endoscope from below, craniotomy from above), then the endoscopic transnasal craniectomy, removing the skull base and dura through the nose, with no craniotomy. In 2005 Pittsburgh mapped the midline into modules: transcribriform, transplanum, transsellar, transclival, down to the cervicomedullary junction.
3️⃣ Lateral corridors: wider endoscopic maxillectomies opened routes forward (Sturmann–Canfield type, often called "endoscopic Denker") and back to the pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae — for angiofibromas, schwannomas, chordomas and selected cancers. In 2017 Varese (Turri-Zanoni, 5 types, 1,378 patients) and Brescia (Schreiber, types A–D) classified these maxillectomies; the two systems largely correspond.

Less morbidity: no facial incisions, less blood loss; in selected low-stage cancers, survival similar to open surgery.

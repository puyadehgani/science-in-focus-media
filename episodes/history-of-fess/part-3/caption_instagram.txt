How did the nose become a doorway to the skull base? 🧠 Part 3/5 · History of FESS

From the microscope to the endoscope, and from the sella to the skull base:

1️⃣ The microscope (1960s): transsphenoidal surgery through a speculum, with a straight, narrow view.
2️⃣ The endoscope as an aid: looking around corners the microscope could not see (Paris 1963, Los Angeles 1977, Germany 1978, Egypt 1994–95, Vienna 1995).
3️⃣ Hybrid: the endoscope for one phase, the microscope for the rest.
4️⃣ The endoscope alone: in the 1990s, ENT surgeons and neurosurgeons in Europe, Asia and the Americas worked fully endoscopically — among the documented centres Nancy (1992), Pittsburgh (from 1993), Singapore (1995), Syracuse NY (1996), Naples (from 1997; "functional endoscopic pituitary surgery", FEPS, 1998), Bologna (from 1998) and Fortaleza, Brazil (from 1998).
5️⃣ Expanded endonasal approaches in the 2000s: Pittsburgh (2005), with Bologna and Naples in parallel.

Meanwhile, rhinologists closed CSF leaks and encephaloceles endoscopically: a 2000 meta-analysis of 289 leaks found 90% closed at the first attempt.
Larger openings needed better closure: the vascularised nasoseptal flap (Hadad & Bassagasteguy, Rosario, Argentina, with Pittsburgh, 2006). A 2012 review: postoperative leaks 15.6% with free grafts vs 6.7% with vascularised flaps.

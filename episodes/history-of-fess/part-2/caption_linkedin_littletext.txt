How did one sinus operation conquer the world?
Science in Focus · History of FESS · Part 2/5

Functional endoscopic sinus surgery did not spread through technique alone. Four things made it global:

• Organised rhinology: the American Rhinologic Society \(1954\), the European Rhinologic Society \(1963, Leiden\), the International Rhinologic Society \(1965, Kyoto\) and ISIAN \(1976, Tokyo\).
• Structured teaching: Kennedy's courses at Johns Hopkins from 1985 and Stammberger's in Graz from 1986.
• Technology: image-guided navigation, first used in surgery in 1987 \(Schlöndorff, Aachen\); powered instrumentation \(1994\); balloon dilation of the sinus ostia \(2006\).
• A shared language to measure outcomes: the Lund-Mackay CT score \(1993\), the Lund-Kennedy endoscopic score \(1995\), standardised smell testing, the SNOT-22, validated on 3,128 patients in the UK national audit \(2009\), and the EPOS and international consensus papers.

Access, however, remains unequal. In 15 sub-Saharan African countries, the number of ENT surgeons rose by 43% between 2009 and 2015, while the population grew by 23%; respondents named the lack of basic equipment as the main limitation.
